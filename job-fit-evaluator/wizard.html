JobFit setup

    Welcome to JobFit

    JobFit reads a job posting and tells you how well it fits you, with a score, what you match, what you're missing, and whether the pay is in your range.

    You choose where postings are scored. With LM Studio, a model runs on this computer and nothing is sent anywhere. With the OpenAI API, there's nothing to install, but postings and your profile are sent to OpenAI.

    Setup takes about five minutes. You'll need:

    • LM Studio running with a model loaded, or an OpenAI API key (a later step checks either for you)
    • Your CV or LinkedIn profile, as text to paste

    Moving from another computer? (Tracked jobs → Data).

    Language and location

    JobFit works in your language, and where you are fills in the next steps for you. Both are optional and stay on this computer.

    Where you live

    Stored only in this browser. The model is told your city only if you allow it on the Work preferences step.

    About you

    A profile is one person's settings. The countries you apply in and your right to work in each decide which postings are rejected automatically — you can review that on the Hard rejects step.

    Which countries are you applying in?
    Only the Americas for now. Postings elsewhere are still scored; they just don't get country-specific rules.

    Work preferences

    These never reject a posting. They add warnings you can judge for yourself, like an on-site job in another city or working hours far from your time zone.

    Which work arrangements would you accept?
    Would you relocate at your own cost?
    Which languages can you work in?
    A posting that requires another language gets a warning.

    Your candidate profile

    A short summary of your experience. Every posting is scored against it, so it needs to be specific and honest, including what you haven't done.

    This is the model's draft. Read it through: correct anything it got wrong, and make sure Gaps lists what you really haven't done.
    Add a Gaps: line. The evaluator uses it to tell a real gap from something you just didn't mention. Without it, most postings score too high.

    Expected salary

    Shown next to each posting's pay. It doesn't set the score directly, but a posting whose ceiling is far below your floor is flagged as probably below your level.

    Which currencies are you paid in?
    From the countries you apply in. Remote roles for US companies are often paid in USD.

    Hard rejects

    A posting that says any of these is rejected immediately, without asking the model. Only tick what truly rules a job out for you.

    Citizenship, clearance and sponsorship rules follow your answers on About you: they only reject a posting in a country where they apply to you. When a posting doesn't say where it is, they become a warning instead.
    Advanced: raw patterns

    Warnings

    These are highlighted in amber for you to judge. Unlike hard rejects, they never block an evaluation or change the score.

    Advanced: raw patterns

    Domain flags

    Skills and fields you don't have. When a posting mentions one, it's shown straight away and the model has to check it. If the job really requires it, the score is capped at 50.

    Your flags
    For example: machine learning, Kubernetes, React Native. "C++" and ".NET" work as written. Add the term in each language your postings use.

    You're set up

    The model isn't connected yet, so evaluations won't run until it is.
    1. Open a job posting (LinkedIn, Greenhouse, Indeed, Workday and most career sites work).
    2. Click the JobFit icon in the toolbar, then Evaluate this tab.
    3. The score appears on the page. Every job you score is kept in Tracked jobs.
